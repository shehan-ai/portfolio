Educational Video — Mycelium Leather

Project Title — Sustainable Biotechnology: Mycelium Leather as an Eco-Friendly Alternative

Group Members

Buwanaji Nagahawatta, Vihara Yasassrini, Nanduni Senaratne, Manuth Dharmasena

Assignment Description

Objective: Communicate a complex biotechnology concept to a broader audience through multimedia, demonstrating technical accuracy, creative storytelling, and teamwork.

Task: Concept development, scripting, on-camera presentation, and video editing for an educational video on fungal biotechnology—specifically mycelium leather—explaining its structural biology, production process, and environmental benefits over traditional leather.

Summary

​For Assignment 2, Group 1 produced an educational video exploring mycelium leather—a biomaterial produced from the vegetative root structure of fungi (mycelium) as a sustainable alternative to animal leather and synthetic plastics. Working alongside Vihara, Nanduni, and Manuth, I scripted and presented the introductory segment and the structural overview of mycelium bio-fabrication. ​To ensure high production quality and professional engagement, we filmed key segments on location at the Independence Square Arcade and across the SLIIT campus. A core feature of our video project was an exclusive interview with our module lecturer, Prof. Colin, who provided expert academic insights into the future commercialization of fungal biomaterials. We combined live-action presentation, laboratory footage, graphic overlays, and structural diagrams to break down how fungal hyphae branch into dense micro-networks that can be harvested, dried, and treated into durable leather substitutes.

Reflection

Creating the mycelium leather video assignment was an enriching exercise in collaborative learning, project coordination, and science communication. Scripting the technical aspects of fungal biotechnology forced me to translate complex biological mechanisms into engaging, accessible language for viewers. ​Filming at public and campus locations taught me valuable practical skills in staging, vocal delivery, and camera management under real-world conditions. Securing and conducting the interview with Prof. Colin strengthened my professional interviewing and communication skills. Working closely with Vihara, Nanduni, and Manuth built strong team cohesion, as we balanced scriptwriting, filming logistics, and post-production editing to deliver a polished educational project.

Career Trajectory & Role Model

Project Title — My Biotechnology Role Model: Professor Sangeeta Bhatia – Scientist, Inventor, Entrepreneur

Role Model

Professor Sangeeta Bhatia (B.S. Brown University, M.S. & Ph.D. MIT, M.D. Harvard Medical School)

Assignment Description

Objective: Explore professional career pathways in biotechnology, investigate the contributions of a pioneering biotechnologist, and map personal career ambitions against industry developments and academic progression.

Task: Conduct literature research using institutional resources and academic portals to profile a prominent biotechnology role model and construct a multi-stage personal career development roadmap.

Summary

​​For Assignment 3, I conducted research using the SLIIT campus library facilities to present a career trajectory profile on Professor Sangeeta Bhatia, a pioneering scientist, inventor, professor, and biotechnology entrepreneur. Prof. Bhatia holds degrees from Brown University (B.S. in Biomedical Engineering), MIT (M.S. & Ph.D. in Engineering, Medicine, and Biology), and Harvard Medical School (M.D.). I selected Prof. Bhatia because she bridges scientific research with commercial translation, connects engineering with medicine, and inspires women in STEM and biotech entrepreneurship. ​Her major scientific contributions include developing engineered human liver models like HepatoPac®️ (micropatterned hepatocyte islands used for toxicity testing and drug development), tissue engineering, cancer research, disease detection, and nanosensors. Over her career, she has received honors including the Lemelson-MIT Prize ($500,000 "Oscar for Inventors"), the 20th Heinz Award, the Othmer Gold Medal, and the Packard Fellowship. ​Inspired by her journey, I formulated my three-stage career roadmap: ​Short Term: Build core biotechnology knowledge, practical lab skills, and effective communication at SLIIT, leading to industry internships. ​Medium Term: Pursue postgraduate education, gain hands-on industry experience, and develop business and leadership skills. ​Long Term: Evolve into a biotechnology leader driving bioproducts entrepreneurship and translating scientific research into impactful societal solutions.

Reflection

Completing Assignment 3 was a test of resilience and time management. Due to personal constraints, I had a very tight window to prepare for this presentation, leaving me with only seven days to rehearse my delivery and slides. Despite these time pressures, I utilized the SLIIT library e-resources effectively to gather accurate, high-quality information and committed to intensive practice sessions throughout the week. ​Achieving a mark of 75 proved that disciplined effort and efficient research can yield strong results under pressure. Studying Professor Sangeeta Bhatia’s career reinforced that modern biotechnology extends far beyond laboratory benchwork. Her multi-disciplinary training across engineering, medicine, and business demonstrates that solving complex global health challenges requires an integrative approach. Mapping out her accomplishments alongside my own goals provided a clear blueprint for my professional growth at SLIIT.

A library-based study profiling Professor Sangeeta Bhatia as a biotechnology role model, analyzing her bio-entrepreneurship model and mapping a personal short-, medium-, and long-term career roadmap. Completed under strict preparation time using SLIIT Library e-resources.

Bioethics Debate — Forensic DNA Databases

Debate Motion: "This house believes that law enforcement should be allowed to use consumer DNA databases to track down suspects through their genetic relatives."

​

Role: Opposing / Negative Team Leader (Buwanaji Nagahawatta - HS26200917)

​

Opposition Team: Buwanaji Nagahawatta (Leader), Yashoda, and Yasith

​

Affirmative Team: Randika (Leader), Dulneth, and Saranya

​

Date & Time: September 2, 2026 | 1:30 PM – 5:00 PM

​

Venue & Lecturer: Hall G1106 | Lecturer: Mr. Shanitha M

Outcome

Victory for the Opposition Team (Won the majority vote from the audience)

Assignment Description

Objective: ​Critically evaluate a controversial topic in modern biotechnology, articulate structured arguments during a formal academic debate, and reflect on the ethical, legal, and social responsibilities of scientists.

Task: Conduct deep literature and database research using the SLIIT campus library e-resources and academic web portals to analyze forensic genetic genealogy. Prepare opening statements, real-time counter-arguments, and closing arguments under strict time limits for a formal academic debate.

Summary

​​On September 2, 2026, our team participated in an intense academic debate in Hall G1106 under the supervision of Mr. Shanitha M. Serving as the Opposition Team Leader alongside Yashoda and Yasith, I led our argument against the Affirmative motion presented by Randika (Affirmative Leader), Dulneth, and Saranya. ​To build a strong defense, our team conducted deep background research using the SLIIT Library digital databases—such as ScienceDirect and PubMed—as well as legal-forensic case studies online. This enabled us to analyze key bioethical precedents, terms of service in direct-to-consumer (DTC) databases like GEDmatch, and the mechanics of long-range familial DNA searching. Combined with seven consecutive days of Zoom rehearsals and lecture hall practice sessions, our research allowed us to prove that opening consumer databases to police creates an unregulated biological dragnet. We exposed the severe loss of genetic privacy for non-consenting relatives and highlighted technical vulnerabilities like secondary DNA transfer. Our research-backed arguments persuaded the room, earning our team the majority of student votes.

Reflection

Leading the Opposition Team to win the audience vote was a highlight of my introductory biotechnology coursework at SLIIT. Utilizing the SLIIT eLibrary to access peer-reviewed journals helped us ground our speeches in real scientific and legal evidence rather than mere opinion. ​The process demonstrated the value of combining academic research with structured practice. Synthesizing literature on forensic genetics during our seven days of Zoom practice gave us the rapid-recall capability needed to counter the Affirmative team's points during the live session in Hall G1106. Beyond the debate itself, this assignment deepened my appreciation for bioethics, demonstrating that scientific advances must always be evaluated through the lens of human rights, user consent, and legal responsibility.
